# Architecture

```
            Ascend launchpad (Infinity pool hook)
                 │ creator fees, 50% of the 1% swap fee, both assets
                 ▼
  dev ──transferCreator──▶ Lodestar (one clone per token) ◀── distribute() by anyone
                 │
     token fees ─┼─▶ Exile.notifyStakeReward ─▶ 3 day drip in the token itself (tokenToHolders, the
                 │   default), or burned (0xdEAD…2069; Ascend tokens reject 0x…dEaD) if the creator chose to
                 │
     quote fees ─┼─ burnBps ────▶ AscendSwapper ─▶ buy token ─▶ burned
                 ├─ holdersBps ─▶ Exile.notifyReward ─▶ 3 day drip to lockers by points
                 └─ treasuryBps ▶ Sandfall.topUp ─▶ rolling stream to the Team Treasury
```

## Lodestar

- **Activation.** `LodestarFactory.predict(token, quote)` gives the router address before it exists (EIP-1167 clone, salt = Ascend pool id). The dev calls Ascend's `transferCreator(poolId, router)`, then `factory.deploy(params)`, which in one transaction clones the router, creates its Exile reward pool and Sandfall treasury stream, initializes it, calls `acceptCreator`, optionally enrols the listing set-aside and optionally locks the creator role. `deploy` requires the caller to be the pool's current creator, so nobody can front-run the address with their own settings. The dev keeps the dev-buy tokens; only the fee stream moves.
- **Routing.** `distribute()` tries `collect` (Ascend's keeper may already have collected), tries `claim`, routes all token-side balance (paid to Exile lockers in full when the config's `tokenToHolders` is set, as the site and the activation script do by default, or burned), then splits the quote balance (minus any buyback reserve) by the configured basis points. Rounding remainders go to the treasury (or to holders, then buyback, when the treasury share is 0). It routes by balance, not by return values, so fees claimed by someone else or sent directly are routed too. Every outside call is isolated: if collect, claim, the token burn or token payout, the EMA read, the buyback, the Exile payout or the treasury top-up fails, that amount stays in the router (in `buybackReserve`, `holdersReserve` or `treasuryReserve`) and is retried next time while the other steps still run. Before paying holders it catches the Exile pool up with one bounded `poke` (`EXILE_CATCH_UP`, 720 hours); a pool further behind than that parks both holder payouts for that call, and every call moves it on, so a pool weeks behind can't push `distribute` past a block's gas. A call sent with too little gas for that catch-up (a wallet's estimate lands there) reverts with `CatchUpOutOfGas` instead of parking the payouts without moving the pool. The keeper and the site's Distribute card catch a far-behind pool up with `poke` first.
- **Buyback safety.** The buyback's minimum output is the stricter of two floors, one at Ascend's on-chain EMA price and one at the pool's spot price, less the 1% pool fee and the configured slippage (default 10%, min 1%, max 20%). Spot alone is never trusted, since a sandwich can move it in the same transaction, so with no EMA the buyback waits (Ascend sets the EMA at launch, so in practice there always is one); but spot can only tighten the floor, so a dump that leaves the EMA stale and high can't loosen it either. Each buyback spends at most 0.5% of the quote side of the pool's active liquidity, valued at whichever of the EMA and spot prices gives the smaller depth, so it moves the price about 1%, less than the 1% fee a sandwich pays on each leg. With no active liquidity (a price below Ascend's first band, as in a fresh pool or a token sold all the way back) there is no depth to size the buy against, so it waits rather than going out uncapped. Buybacks are also at least 30 minutes apart (`BUYBACK_INTERVAL`): the cap bounds one buy, and without a gap a caller could push several capped pieces through in one block between their own buy and sell. The keeper runs every 3 hours, so the gap costs nothing in the normal flow. Bigger amounts wait in `buybackReserve` and go out in pieces on later distributes. After a skipped attempt the next one halves (down to 1/256), at most once every 30 minutes, and each success doubles it back. Only a miss a smaller attempt could fix counts: attempts that were never tried (no EMA, no active liquidity), dust attempts, and misses explained by an EMA still trailing a pump (its floor asks for more than a swap at spot pays even with no price impact, so no size gets through) don't halve anything. So neither a quiet day after a launch pump, nor a few wei, nor a burst of calls during one pump can shrink the next real buyback. Each buyback is checked by what actually happened: at most the attempted quote left the router and at least the minimum output reached the burn address. A swapper the steward picks could still keep the margin between the fair price and that minimum (up to the fee plus slippage), which is why swapper changes sit behind the timelock. The EMA only moves on swaps, so after a large trade it can trail spot by a few percent; a tighter setting (such as 3%) makes buybacks wait for it to catch up, which is why the default is 10%.
- **Governance.** One steward (the dev). Split, swapper, slippage and token-side route changes go through `proposeConfig` and can be executed by anyone after 12 hours. `lockCreatorRole(until)` can only extend and is refused while a hand-off is pending, so a pending transfer can't be accepted after the lock; `transferCreatorOut` is blocked while locked, except `transferCreatorOut(0)` to cancel. The factory locks the role at activation when `creatorLockUntil` is set; the site and the activation script lock it forever (`type(uint64).max`) unless the creator unticks the box or sets `LOCK_FOREVER=false`. Even a forever lock has one way out, an upgrade: the factory owner names a newer factory with `setSuccessor`, the steward calls `proposeUpgrade` on the router, which records that factory's predicted router for the token, and anyone can call `executeUpgrade` after `UPGRADE_DELAY` (7 days) and within the 7 days after, which starts Ascend's hand-off to that router whatever the lock. The factory owner is set at deployment and changes hands in two steps (`transferOwner`, then `acceptOwner` from the new owner); on a live chain `DeployCore` requires `FACTORY_OWNER` to be a contract (a multisig, separate from every steward) unless `ALLOW_EOA_OWNER=true`, so no single key can name a successor. `executeUpgrade` reverts if the factory has named a different successor since, the steward can cancel before or (with `transferCreatorOut(0)`) after it runs until the new router accepts, and locked tokens and vesting streams in Exile and Sandfall are untouched. If the role ever leaves (an expired lock, a hand-out the steward reverses), the current creator hands it back on Ascend and anyone can call `acceptCreator` to complete the return; Ascend's own two-step is the consent. The router is also the admin of its Exile reward pool, so tier changes go through `proposeTiers` and `cancelTiers` on the router and follow `transferSteward`. There is no sweep or rescue function: funds can only go to the burn address, Exile, Sandfall or the swapper. The factory checks that token and quote weren't passed the wrong way round, refuses a second deploy for a pool cheaply instead of burning its gas on the CREATE2 collision, and a new factory default swapper only applies to deploys 12 hours later.
- **Listing set-aside.** Enrolling routes 20% of the creator's quote share to Ascend's ListingDesk toward a HyperCore listing, up to a cap. Ascend clears an election on creator change, so the factory enrols right after the router takes over. The site and the activation script leave it off, so the creator's whole quote share reaches the router; a router that enrolled earlier can leave from the steward controls. If Ascend lists the token anyway, `splitCoreFees` pays the creator half of its HyperCore fees to the router, and `distribute` routes whatever arrives in the token or its quote asset.

## Exile

- Points = amount × tier multiplier, chosen by the time still committed: from now to the unlock time, both when locking and when extending. Keeping Monarch means keeping 30 days ahead; a lock can't hold 4x through one-hour extensions. Tiers must not pay a longer lock less than a shorter one.
- Rewards accrue Synthetix-style per second, shared by points. Each `notifyReward` drips over 3 days, so a lock taken right before a payout only catches a sliver. When a payout lands mid-drip, the new end is weighted by amount: what is still dripping keeps its remaining time, and the payout plus anything that dripped while nobody was locked get a full 3 days. Dust barely moves the end and splitting a payout changes nothing.
- Each pool has two reward streams on the same points: the reward token (the quote asset, from `notifyReward`) and the stake token itself (token-side fees, from `notifyStakeReward`, when the creator chose to pay them to lockers). Both follow the same drip, carry and unlock rules and one claim pays both; `claimStakeRewardsTo` and `claimRewardTokenTo` each pay one stream alone, so a token that can't be paid out right now (paused) doesn't hold the other one hostage. Stake-token rewards are accounted separately from locked principal: withdraw returns exactly the amount locked, and rewards are only ever paid from what was notified. The stake token must not charge transfer fees or rebase.
- Unlock times near the uint64 limit are rejected rather than rounded, so they can't wrap into the past.
- Unlock times round up to the hour. Points expiring in each hour are kept in a bucket, and the pool snapshots the reward index at each hour boundary it passes, so a lock stops earning exactly at unlock even if nobody touches it. One action catches up at most 720 hours, and at most 300 of the hours in which points expire (each of those snapshots both streams), which keeps the worst case at about 18.5M gas. A pool further behind can be caught up with `poke`. Payouts called directly (`notifyReward`, `notifyStakeReward`) catch up without either cap, and the caller pays for it. Lodestar instead pokes first, one capped call per `distribute`, and parks its holder payouts while the pool is further behind, so dust locks expiring hour after hour can delay holder payouts until the pool is caught up but can't make a `distribute` sent with enough gas revert.
- Tier changes are timelocked 12 hours and must be executed within 7 days after that. They apply to new locks and to extensions. A tier can't be shorter than one hour or pay more than 5x, so an admin can't add a tier that farms the drip with capital committed for seconds. For a Lodestar token the pool admin is the router, so tier changes belong to whoever is steward. NFT locks hold the item and earn nothing; an ERC-721 address is refused on the token path so it can't be stranded there. Rewards can be claimed to another address (`claimRewardsTo`) in case the reward token refuses the owner's wallet.
- The team runs a keeper (`.github/workflows/distribute.yml`) that calls `distribute` every 3 hours and pays the gas. Small, steady payouts keep the drip rate nearly flat, so there is no pile of pending fees worth timing a lock for, and each buyback stays small next to the depth cap and the EMA guard.
- A reward pool further behind than one catch-up makes lock, claim, extend and withdraw on its locks wait for a `poke` (permissionless, chunked). Lodestar's `distribute` keeps the pool current, one capped catch-up per call; while the pool is further behind, or if a payout fails, the holder share waits in `holdersReserve` (and token-side fees bound for lockers stay in the router) instead of blocking the other steps.

## Sandfall

- Fixed streams: linear or steps with an optional cliff. Steps count from `start`, not the cliff, and a short last step pays a full equal share. Rolling streams start empty; on each `topUp` the new end is weighted by amount: what was still vesting keeps its remaining time and the new amount gets a full window (Lodestar's treasury uses 7 days). Splitting a top-up into pieces changes nothing.
- Use non-rebasing tokens: balances are measured once, at deposit.
- `claimTo` lets a recipient that can't receive the asset (no native receive, or blocked by the token) claim to another address.
- `cancellable` is fixed at creation. Cancelling returns the unvested rest to the creator; the vested part stays claimable by the recipient, so a recipient that can't receive can't block a cancel. In the `Cancelled` event, `toRecipient` is the amount left claimable, not paid.
- Anyone can create a rolling stream naming any creator, so the site should list a project's streams from the ids stored in its Lodestar, not from `createdBy`.

## Other launchpads: adapters and native HYPE

Everything launchpad specific sits behind `IFeeSource`, one stateless adapter per launchpad (`contracts/src/sources/`): `AscendFeeSource`, and for the Uniswap v4 launchpads on Elysium mainnet `EleosFeeSource`, `KleosFeeSource` and `BoxaFeeSource` on a shared `V4FeeSource`. Each factory is built for one adapter and hands it to every router it makes; the router stores it at `initialize` with the coin's hook and never changes either. The adapter answers views (creator, pending creator, what is owed, the holder share, the dividend claim, the swap cost, prices) and lists calls (collect, claim, accept, transfer out); the router sends those calls itself, because the launchpad only answers the address holding the creator role. A listed call must target the router's stored hook and carries no value, so a wrong adapter answer can at worst fail a step, which `distribute` already tolerates.

- **Handover.** `handover()` says whether the launchpad is two step (Ascend, Boxa: propose, then the factory's `deploy` accepts) or one step (Eleos, Kleos: `deploy` first, then the creator calls `transferCreator`). On a one-step launchpad nothing on chain proves who sent a role that arrived before the router existed, so `deploy` refuses it and the factory owner can finish it with `rescueDeploy(params, steward)` after checking off chain. The same call recovers a role sent to another coin's router from the same factory: it deploys the coin's router and has the wrong router pass the role on (`releaseForeignRole`, factory only, never the router's own coin).
- **Hooks.** A v4 coin names its own hook (`coin.hook()`); the adapter only accepts hooks in its registry, which its owner extends with `addHook(hook, kind)` and can never edit or shrink. The kind picks the claim and pool layout for that hook generation.
- **Native HYPE.** When the quote is `address(0)` the router uses its HYPE balance, pays Exile with `notifyReward{value}`, Sandfall with `topUp{value}` and the swapper with `buyExactIn{value}`, and has a `receive()` that only accepts. Exile reward pools and Sandfall streams for these routers use `address(0)` as their asset; Exile pays HYPE claims with a plain call and reverts `PayoutFailed` (the reward stays) for a recipient that refuses it, so `claimRewardsTo` exists for that case.
- **Dividend harvest.** The v4 launchpads pay holders HYPE by balance and don't exclude Exile, so Exile earns dividends on every locked coin. A registrar (each v4 factory, added behind a 12 hour timelock) sets `harvestOf[coin]` at activation to one of three shapes: `coin.claimFor(exile)`, `coin.claimRewards(exile)` or `hook.claimRewards(coin, exile)`. `harvest(coin)` is permissionless; it measures the HYPE that arrived during the claim, adds what the coin pushed to Exile outside a harvest (`unattributed[coin]`), and notifies it to the coin's pool. HYPE from any other sender is kept apart and never harvested. Because a harvest pays the dividends on all of Exile's balance of the coin to one pool, a coin with a harvest only takes locks in that pool: `lockERC20` refuses a plain lock or a lock in another pool of it (`HarvestPoolOnly`). A coin that pays holders a share Exile can't claim is refused at deploy (`HoldersSplitActive`).
- **Prices.** `V4PriceObserver` keeps one time-weighted average of `sqrtPriceX96` per pool over a 30 minute window, with at most one observation per second, so a swap and a buyback in one transaction can't move the average that prices that buyback. It reads pool state straight from the pool manager's storage (`V4State`, the StateLibrary layout behind `extsload`) and returns 0 until it has watched a pool for a full window. The buyback floor subtracts the adapter's `swapCostBps` (the coin's buy tax) instead of Ascend's fixed 1% fee, and a cost above `MAX_SWAP_COST_BPS` (5%) skips the buyback.

## V4Swapper

Exact HYPE-in buys on a Uniswap v4 pool: `poolManager.unlock` → `unlockCallback` → `swap(zeroForOne, -amountIn, MIN_SQRT_PRICE + 1, empty hook data)`, then `settle{value}` for the HYPE actually taken and `take(coin, to, out)` straight to the burn sink, so the router never holds the coin and a max-wallet limit can't block it. Whatever the pool didn't take is refunded. It looks the pool key up in the observer and refuses a pool not quoted in native HYPE. One per pool manager; it holds nothing between calls.

## AscendSwapper

Exact-input buys and sells on Ascend pools through PancakeSwap Infinity: `vault.lock` → `lockAcquired` → `CLPoolManager.swap`, then `sync`/`transfer`/`settle` for the input and `take` for the output. Holds nothing between calls.

## Tests

`forge test` runs 231 tests: unit tests for each contract, native HYPE routing, one-step activation and rescue, every harvest shape, the v4 pieces on a mock pool manager, AscendSwapper in both currency orderings, the Ascend pool-id test vector from a real testnet launch, a full integration test on mock Ascend, property fuzz tests (`Fuzz.t.sol`) and stateful invariant suites for Exile, Sandfall and Lodestar (`invariants/`). The mocks reproduce the Ascend behaviour the contracts depend on: creator fee split, pull-based claim to the current creator, the two-step creator handover that clears the listing election, the 20% set-aside with carry and cap, and Infinity's lock/settle/take with zero-delta enforcement. The fork suites in `contracts/test/fork/` (29 tests, skipped without `ELYSIUM_RPC_URL`) run the Eleos, Kleos and Boxa adapters against the real hooks and pool managers on an Elysium mainnet fork; see [launchpads.md](launchpads.md).
